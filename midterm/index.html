The World of Dolphins

Dolphins are a very socially active and highly intelligent marine mammal known for their playful demeanor. They use a variety of clicks, whistles, and body movements to communicate; this is a very complex network of sounds but can be as specific as having individual names for each other. Dolphins breathe air, give birth to live calf, and produce milk which is why they are mammals. One of the more popular species is the Bottlenose Dolphin which is usually recognized for having a curved smile. This is also the species most often seen in aquariums, for public entertainment, and dolphin pools, where humans can swim with them. Dolphins are a cooperative animal; they use their intelligence to work together in the wild to hunt prey and protect each other when being hunted. This collaborative nature also comes into play with training for human interaction. Marine scientists and the public have always had a strong fascination with dolphins, and if we work to protect them from extinction, they will be enjoyed for many years to come.

Fun Facts

  • Dolphins use echolocation to analyze sounds around them to determine what objects they are near.
  • Only half of a dolphin's brain shuts down to sleep, while the other half stays alert.
  • While swimming, dolphins can reach speeds of roughly 30-50 km per hour.
  • Dolphins can have anywhere from 2 to 250 teeth, depending on the species.
